FLAGGED EXERCISES:

  ? good_morning — primary: Mid Back, Lats
     The good morning is primarily a hip hinge that targets the posterior
     chain. Listing it under Back is defensible (spinal erectors/back
     involvement), but many coaches classify it as a hamstring/glute
     exercise. The dictionary has a separate "good_morning_hamstring"
     variant to address this. The dual listing approach is acceptable.
     VERDICT: ACCEPTABLE (with the hamstring variant covering the gap)

  ? deadlift_barbell — primary: Lats, Mid Back, Traps
     The conventional deadlift is classified as a back exercise, which
     is a common powerlifting perspective. However, it involves every
     posterior chain muscle nearly equally. The secondary weights of
     Glutes 0.7 and Hamstrings 0.7 acknowledge this.
     VERDICT: ACCEPTABLE — placing under Back is a reasonable convention.

  ? deadlift_trap_bar — primary: Lats, Mid Back
     Trap bar deadlifts are MORE quad-dominant than conventional deadlifts,
     yet they are classified under Back. The Quads 0.7 secondary weight
     reflects this, but some would argue Quads should be co-primary.
     VERDICT: BORDERLINE — consider adding Quads to primaryMuscles, or
     accept the 0.7 secondary as adequate compensation.

  ? rack_pull — primary: Traps, Mid Back, Lats
     Rack pulls are primarily a back/traps exercise. CORRECT.

  ? hyperextension — primary: Lower Back
     Some coaches classify hyperextensions as glute/hamstring exercises,
     especially with a hip-hinge execution. The dictionary lists
     Glutes 0.5 and Hamstrings 0.4 as secondaries, which is reasonable.
     VERDICT: ACCEPTABLE

  ? "Upper Traps" — normalizes to Back (via "trap" substring match).
     This means OHP secondary volume for Upper Traps counts toward Back,
     not Delts. This is anatomically defensible but may surprise users
     who associate traps with shoulder day.

  ? "Adductors" — normalizes to Glutes.
     Adductors are a distinct muscle group but are grouped with Glutes
     for tracking purposes. This is a common simplification.

  ? "External Rotators" — normalizes to Delts (via the rotator/external
     rotator catch-all). Face pull secondary volume for external rotators
     counts toward Delts. This is a stretch but acceptable for simplicity.

  ? "Shoulders" (raw value on landmine_twist secondary) — normalizes to
     Delts. This is correct.

  ? "Lower Back" — normalizes to Back. This means hyperextension and
     reverse hyperextension primary volume counts toward Back. Correct
     for volume tracking purposes.

  ? "Brachialis" and "Brachioradialis" — both normalize to Biceps.
     This means hammer curl and reverse curl secondary volume for
     biceps is being double-counted: once for the "Biceps" secondary
     and again implicitly from primary "Brachialis" normalizing to Biceps.
     For hammer curls: primary Brachialis (-> Biceps 1.0) + secondary
     Biceps 0.5 = the exercise counts as both 1.0 + 0.5 = 1.5 bicep
     sets? This depends on implementation — if primaryMuscles and
     secondaryMuscles are processed independently, this is correct
     behavior (the primary IS the brachialis which normalizes to Biceps,
     and there is additional bicep head involvement at 0.5).
     VERDICT: Technically correct but worth noting for volume tracking.
